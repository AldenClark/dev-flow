# Dev Flow 2.0 RC.3 requirements

## Outcome

Dev Flow 2.0 RC.3 makes the quality kernel reliable across real multi-turn repository work while adding a maintainable repository-knowledge capability. It must improve activation, transition, recovery, scope convergence, method realization, outcome persistence, and evidence freshness without turning ordinary work into a persisted workflow or expanding the methodology pool.

Implementation is convergent by default: reasoning may become deeper inside the confirmed boundary, but exploration or mutation becomes broader only when the user explicitly requests exploration or approves a material expansion. Long-running autonomy remains persistence toward the authorized outcome, never authority expansion.

The release is an RC qualification step, not a stable-2.0 claim. Stable promotion remains a separate decision based on real-task soak evidence.

## Confirmed context

- RC.2 substantially improved high-risk design, implementation, verification, and independent-review honesty.
- Real RC.2 tasks still exposed missed implicit activation, missed re-routing after intent changes, raw CLI parse failures, repeated unavailable-tool attempts, and very large task lineages whose final evidence was difficult to bind to the latest worktree.
- The existing deterministic and model-semantic suites validate routing branches, but they do not fully validate host discovery, multi-turn transitions, capability failure recovery, or task-fork continuity.
- Repository knowledge must serve people and agents through progressive disclosure rather than a large generated AGENTS.md.
- Repository knowledge establishment and release orchestration have different authority and failure boundaries. They must remain separate capabilities.
- A product-owned release workflow is the first proving ground for release orchestration. One product is insufficient evidence for a generic Dev Flow release engine.
- A privacy-minimized audit of 50 locally visible Codex tasks and 26 ordinary ChatGPT conversations found that multi-turn intent changes, context compaction, explicit continuation, repeated attempts, and cross-task references are normal usage shapes. The ordinary conversations are negative controls, not a reason to make Dev Flow a universal assistant workflow.
- A focused review of eight deduplicated high-complexity task lineages found repeated user corrections that narrowed exploration, separated reference material from requirements, constrained platforms or changed paths, or moved an adjacent problem to another task. Scope drift is therefore a real behavior gap rather than a hypothetical policy concern.
- Existing scope checkpoints and delegated owned-path briefs are necessary but incomplete: they do not yet distinguish reasoning depth from exploration breadth, carry non-goals through compaction and delegation, or give range-external findings one consistent disposition.
- The methodology registry is structurally mature at 117 methods, 73 sources, and 38 risk models, but a focused post-RC.2 audit found a runtime realization gap: among 11 completed post-RC.2 tasks, seven invoked the task route, six produced a method selection, only one exposed ready methods, and five produced blocked-only guidance. This is sanitized behavioral evidence, not a productivity or population claim.
- The primary methodology defect is therefore not missing method names. Natural task facts are not reliably translated into the small public activation vocabulary, discovered prerequisites are rarely fed back into selection, blocked fallbacks are easy to ignore, and current tests prove selection mechanics more strongly than method execution or marginal value.

## Actors and goals

### Repository contributor

- Get the smallest relevant engineering process automatically.
- Understand why a route or specialist was selected.
- Continue useful work when one optional capability is unavailable.
- See exactly which evidence is current, stale, blocked, or not run.
- Get deep analysis inside the requested boundary without unrequested platform, repository, dependency, or product expansion.
- Continue an authorized long-running outcome without having to repeat completed work after compaction or interruption.

### Repository owner

- Establish a concise, reviewable knowledge system without overwriting maintained truth or promoting inference into policy.
- Preserve explicit authority over product semantics, document replacement, commit, push, tag, release, deployment, and external communication.
- Resume long work from repository state without depending on a hidden task transcript.
- Keep personal workflow preferences opt-in and local rather than silently promoting observed habits into suite or team policy.
- Use an explicitly referenced task or repository as evidence without allowing stale history or an analogy to become current authority.

### Dev Flow maintainer

- Detect missed and excessive activation using realistic task transitions and negative controls.
- Evolve public route behavior additively where practical and return structured corrections for invalid input.
- Qualify the exact release bytes without treating synthetic routing coverage as business-effect evidence.
- Detect unapproved goal, discovery, mutation, delegation, and evidence-boundary expansion with sanitized positive, negative, and mutation cases.
- Distinguish method activation, selection, realization, and evidence value without rewarding method mentions or call volume.

## Required behavior

### Activation and transition reliability

- Natural material engineering requests must activate Dev Flow without requiring the user to name the plugin.
- A task that begins as research or diagnosis must re-evaluate the kernel when it becomes implementation, review, delivery, cross-repository work, or another material boundary.
- A final-diff audit, assumption-breaking failure, new repository/platform, or imminent delivery action must trigger a bounded recalibration.
- A route remains ephemeral. RC.3 must not introduce a task lifecycle database, activation receipt, packet, lease, or hidden persisted state.
- A narrow lookup, local diagnosis with a known oracle, or one-line mechanical change must remain able to skip the route.

### Route contract

- Invalid values for documented `route-task` value options must return machine-readable output. Unknown flags, missing option values, and other shell/programmer syntax errors may retain argparse diagnostics.
- The explicit alias `diagnosis` must normalize to canonical intent `diagnose`; accepted aliases are allowlisted, not inferred by fuzzy matching. A misspelling such as `diagnoise` returns a bounded suggestion and replayable correction instead of raw usage text.
- A successful route contains only owners required by current facts. On-demand candidates remain a `repo-context` discovery result, and qualified specialist fallbacks remain in the existing capability-activation projection; RC.3 does not add a second route schema without a demonstrated consumer.
- Existing canonical intents, needs, risks, method IDs, and supported output fields remain valid throughout RC.3.

### Capability readiness and failure isolation

- A capability with material prerequisites should be probed before it becomes the sole remaining completion gate.
- An invariant capability failure in the current task environment should be attempted once and not retried until a relevant environment fact changes. This is an agent behavior contract, not a persisted or deterministically enforced circuit-breaker state.
- A blocked capability must not silently become passed, and a same-context fallback must not be described as independent or tool-equivalent evidence.
- Unrelated verification or delivery preparation should continue when safe; the final result reports the isolated blocked gate.

### Scope and evidence freshness

- Managed work must checkpoint when a materially different objective, repository, platform, delivery boundary, or large final-diff exposure appears.
- A checkpoint records completed/current/next outcomes, affected Git roots, user-owned changes, evidence that became stale, blockers, and the recommended next slice.
- Dev Flow may recommend a new task or worktree but must not force one solely from a numeric threshold.
- Final claims must bind to current relevant bytes. Verification performed before later edits cannot remain `PASSED` without an affected-scope freshness check.

### Bounded execution and exploration

- Implementation and defect repair default to a closed discovery boundary. They may inspect the causal code, callers, consumers, configuration, tests, and native evidence required by the confirmed outcome, but they do not acquire repository-wide redesign authority.
- Diagnosis and review default to bounded discovery. They may follow evidence across a necessary causal or contract boundary, but findings outside the requested outcome remain findings until classified and admitted.
- Open exploration is enabled only by an explicit request for requirements discovery, architecture exploration, broad research, alternative generation, or an equivalent breadth instruction. Words such as `deep`, `complete`, `careful`, a high reasoning effort, red/blue analysis, or a request to implement fully do not enable it by themselves.
- The active boundary distinguishes outcome, discovery, mutation, verification, and action/authority. Read scope never silently implies write scope, and implementation authority never silently implies dependency, external, delivery, or delegation authority.
- A newly discovered item is classified as a required defect, necessary enabler, optional opportunity, or unrelated finding. Required defects may proceed inside the confirmed semantics; a necessary enabler that changes public behavior, repository, dependency, platform, data lifecycle, or another material premise requires recalibration and user confirmation. Optional and unrelated findings are reported or deferred, not implemented.
- Reasoning depth and method count may strengthen hypotheses, counterexamples, and evidence only inside the active boundary. They do not add goals, platforms, paths, dependencies, deliverables, or agents.
- Final diff inspection must reject an undeclared write boundary, unexplained dependency, or optional opportunity that entered implementation without admission.

### Method activation and realization

- Treat method use as three separate decisions: whether an additional method can improve the current decision, which smallest ready method or fallback fits the observed failure mechanism, and whether its execution changed an owned artifact or oracle.
- Verification, testing, review, and audit work must make one bounded method disposition when repository evidence exposes a weak/easy-to-fake oracle, behavior preservation, compatibility, state/order/concurrency/recovery, interacting rules, conflicting evidence, repeated failure, model/Skill/agent evaluation, or a material security/privacy/deletion/migration/public-contract consequence.
- A valid disposition is one ready method, an explicit blocked-method fallback, or a reasoned abstention in favor of the owning specialist's sufficient procedure. Selection without a subsequent disposition is incomplete; absence of a method name is not itself a failure.
- Recheck method readiness after repository discovery, material requirement confirmation, the first oracle-breaking failure, repeated non-progress, and before material verification/review. Do not repeat selection when the decision, facts, and prerequisites are unchanged.
- Missing prerequisites remain honest limitations. The route may identify the cheapest safe observation that could establish readiness, but it must not infer a repository fact, independent implementation, stable contract, user example, live environment, or authority merely to make a method ready.
- A selected or fallback method must change at least one decision-useful surface: a test/oracle, counterexample or mutation, state/decision/compatibility model, review attack surface, evidence matrix, or explicit claim limitation. Method selection never authorizes broader discovery, mutation, dependencies, repositories, agents, delivery, or external action.
- Review plus an oracle challenge must be able to surface lower-cost verification methods or fallbacks before a high-cost blocked independent-derivation method consumes the task-facing guidance budget.
- Default to one method. Expose up to three only when they address distinct failure mechanisms and each has a concrete evidence obligation; stop when the method no longer changes a decision or oracle.

### Delegation scope preservation

- Single-agent execution remains the default. Delegation is used only for a bounded independently useful outcome or authorized independent-context value.
- A child receives a subset of the parent's objective, read/write/action authority, resource budget, and stop conditions. Nested delegation can only preserve or narrow that scope.
- Scope expansion, new repository or dependency admission, product-semantics interpretation, and optional-finding promotion remain root/user-owned decisions. A child encountering one stops that branch and returns a bounded expansion request or finding.
- The root reconciles actual reads where observable, actual writes, dependencies, tools, and evidence against the current boundary. A compliant child report cannot excuse an out-of-scope diff.
- Compaction, resume, fork, handoff, or child-result summarization must preserve the active objective, protected behavior, explicit non-goals, discovery mode, write/action boundary, and rejected expansions.

### Semantic continuation and outcome completion

- After compaction, interruption, resume, or fork, the agent reconstructs completed/current/next outcomes from the workstream and current repository facts; reconciles Git roots, user-owned changes, active processes, stale evidence, non-goals, and blockers; and does not treat a conversation summary as repository authority.
- Phrases such as `implement fully`, `keep going`, or `do not stop` authorize persistence toward the already approved terminal condition. They do not authorize commit, push, publication, deployment, destructive action, dependency addition, cross-repository mutation, or a broader product outcome.
- A safe ready slice continues automatically when its semantics, boundary, prerequisites, and evidence are established. Work stops for a genuine blocker, required U1 confirmation, a new material boundary, missing destructive/external authority, or an exhausted predeclared exploration/attempt condition.
- A launched long-running build, test, evaluation, or observation keeps one process/session identity, waits with bounded backoff, preserves the first failure, distinguishes running from failed, and does not restart unchanged work. No babysitting behavior is invented when no process was launched.
- External and runtime facts used after a long pause or resume are rechecked according to their volatility; repository, web, runtime, and release evidence retain separate owners and freshness limits.

### Explicit cross-task and program context

- Cross-task synthesis activates only when the user explicitly references tasks, asks to compare repeated attempts, or authorizes a selected history audit. Dev Flow does not silently search all conversation history during ordinary work.
- Every referenced task is read through the host-supported interface, treated as untrusted historical evidence, and reconciled against current repository truth. The synthesis separates supported claims, stale claims, contradictions, unresolved questions, and current next actions.
- Repeated attempts or forks are not automatically merged or ranked by recency. When explicitly requested, their lineage, different assumptions, failures, and evidence are compared before one current recommendation is formed.
- A repository named as an analogy or reference remains reference evidence. It does not become an authority, owned program member, compatibility promise, or mutation target without explicit scope and repository confirmation.
- A confirmed multi-repository program may record repository roles and contract edges through repository-owned knowledge. A directory containing sibling repositories is not sufficient evidence of program authority.

### Preference adaptation and privacy-safe dogfood

- Stable personal workflow preferences may be proposed through the existing engineering-profile capability only after the user confirms the exact preference, layer, scope, owner, strength, and effect. Historical frequency alone never writes or promotes a preference.
- Personal preferences cannot weaken repository/team must-level controls, native checks, security requirements, or explicit task instructions. Missing optional personal profiles degrade to neutral behavior.
- A maintainer may run an opt-in local dogfood audit over a user-selected corpus when the host exposes an authorized read interface. The audit emits sanitized task shapes, counts, transitions, correction categories, and evidence limits; it does not emit or commit raw transcripts, credentials, absolute personal paths, production data, or stable person/repository scoring.
- If the host cannot provide the selected history safely, the audit is `BLOCKED` or accepts a sanitized observation input. Dev Flow core does not depend on a private host database or automatically retain conversation history.
- Ordinary non-repository conversation, writing, public-information lookup, and daily-life assistance remain negative controls and do not activate Dev Flow.
- Sanitized dogfood distinguishes method-eligible tasks, activation, ready/blocked/fallback/abstain disposition, visible realization, and evidence effect. It does not retain raw transcripts or use method frequency as a person, repository, or quality score.

### Repository knowledge

- Ship `repository-knowledge` as a first-class explicit capability with audit, plan, map, bootstrap, and check modes.
- Keep AGENTS.md concise and path-scoped; route readers to a stable index and the relevant current-truth, rationale, operations, or managed-change owner.
- Keep task maps and inventories replaceable and revision-bound. They never become policy or replace source inspection.
- Distinguish a single repository, manifest-defined monorepo, confirmed multi-repository program, and unversioned workspace container.
- Require explicit approval before replacing maintained documents, creating program-level authority, or promoting inferred behavior into rules.
- Keep repeatable commands in scripts/CLI and enforceable facts in manifests, tests, linters, CI, schemas, or runtime controls.

### Release orchestration boundary

- RC.3 records how a product-owned release Skill and resumable command integrate with Dev Flow, delivery readiness, repository knowledge, and native workflows.
- RC.3 does not ship a generic release state machine based only on one product's workflow.
- Commit, push, tag, workflow rerun/cancel, publication, deployment, and rollback remain distinct actions with distinct authority.
- Product-owned release state may be persisted by the product tool because recovery is part of that product operation; it is not Dev Flow task state.
- Product pilot execution, external repository mutation, and two-release soak evidence are not RC.3 implementation or qualification gates. They require a separately authorized product workstream and remain stable-release inputs.

### Evaluation and qualification

- Add multi-turn semantic cases for diagnosis-to-change, change-to-review, review-to-delivery, capability failure/recovery, new-platform expansion, interruption/resume, and forked-history continuation.
- Add negative controls proving that unchanged follow-up turns do not repeatedly route or reload broad specialists.
- Add mutation tests for missing transition routes, stale verification reuse, raw parser errors, global blocking after an optional capability failure, and false independent-review claims.
- Preserve activation coverage as the release metric. Do not introduce individual productivity tracking, composite quality scores, or incentives to maximize Skill/tool calls.
- Use sanitized task shapes from real dogfood; do not commit private transcripts, credentials, absolute personal paths, or production data.
- Add ordered cases for closed implementation, bounded diagnosis/review, explicitly open exploration, scope-external finding disposition, child and nested-child scope narrowing, non-goal retention after compaction, terminal-condition continuation, long-process supervision, explicit cross-task synthesis, reference-repository handling, and confirmed preference application.
- Add negative controls proving that deep reasoning, red/blue methods, a broad model profile, managed mode, a referenced analogy, or an optional adjacent defect does not by itself expand write scope.
- Add mutation tests that fail when reasoning depth grants breadth, a child broadens delegated authority, a compaction loses non-goals, a referenced task becomes authority, a raw transcript reaches a fixture, an ordinary chat activates Dev Flow, or an unchanged process is restarted.
- Report scope conformance, transition behavior, correction need, and negative-control results separately. Do not turn them into a productivity score or optimize for the fewest files regardless of necessary correctness work.
- Add method-realization cases that independently grade: whether a method check was warranted, whether the selected method/fallback fit the failure mechanism, whether its required output appeared, and whether the final oracle or claim changed.
- Add blocked-to-ready transition, blocked-fallback, reasoned-abstention, and review-to-verification adjacency cases. A mutation that removes the selected method's concrete output or lets a blocked selection disappear without disposition must fail.
- Use paired with-method/without-method trials only for a bounded set of high-value task shapes with fixed repository, requirement, model/tool identity, and deterministic acceptance. Report regressions and overhead beside gains; do not generalize a small convenience sample or make paired trials a source-implementation gate.

## Protected behavior

- Direct mode remains the default for one coherent slice.
- Managed mode remains continuity, not a risk or quality score.
- The process Hook, 1.x packets, evidence ledgers, activation receipts, and mandatory lifecycle states remain absent.
- The methodology pool does not grow in RC.3 unless an implementation failure proves a missing reasoning capability rather than an activation or orchestration defect.
- Method selection count, method-name mentions, context depth, and generated method artifacts are not quality metrics. RC.3 may improve task-facing annotations and realization behavior without creating a method ledger or mandatory report.
- Implementation authority does not imply commit, push, tag, release, deployment, installation, migration execution, or external communication.
- Same-context blue/red work remains useful but is never independent review.
- Deep reasoning is not breadth authority; managed continuity is not open exploration; read access is not mutation authority.
- Cross-task access and personal preference persistence remain explicit and opt-in.
- Scope continuity remains an agent/workstream behavior contract, not a hidden task database or mandatory packet.

## Out of scope

- Stable `2.0.0` publication.
- Automatic rewriting of repositories under a development directory.
- A universal repository document template.
- A generic release engine or GitHub-specific workflow runner in Dev Flow core.
- Persistent telemetry about individual users, repositories, or task outcomes.
- Automatic satisfaction of arbitrary method prerequisites, mandatory method use for ordinary deterministic work, or optimization for maximum method activation. The route may feed back only common prerequisites directly established by its own validated repository-fact and requirement-understanding inputs.
- Product-specific build, signing, packaging, store, or deployment logic.
- Backward compatibility with Dev Flow 1.x runtime state.
- Automatic scanning of future conversations or silent construction of a behavioral profile.
- Automatic task merging, ranking, archiving, closing, or rewriting based on detected similarity.
- A generic runtime authorization service, universal tool proxy, or mandatory persistent scope ledger in RC.3.
- Hard-coded file, token, iteration, or agent-count thresholds as the sole definition of correct scope.
- Product-specific cross-repository topology, personal preference values, or platform implementation rules in the neutral suite.

## Acceptance behavior

RC.3 source implementation is complete only when:

- the route CLI accepts the documented `diagnosis` alias and produces structured, replayable corrections for invalid values in each documented value-option family;
- canonical RC.2 invocations retain field-equivalent route semantics except for the two explicitly approved precision fixes: managed continuity no longer implies requirements, and `persisted-data` alone no longer implies migration;
- deterministic fixture and observation validators reject missing transition turns, forbidden overactivation, capability-failure dishonesty, and stale evidence reuse;
- `repository-knowledge` passes structural, security, topology, link, budget, and real-workspace rehearsal checks;
- deterministic, process-contract, plugin, documentation, security, compilation, and clean-diff gates pass on the final source bytes;
- the opt-in stateful runner has a proven isolated candidate-loading, resume, sanitation, and cleanup path, or the affected claim is explicitly removed/replanned.
- active guidance and deterministic contracts define closed, bounded, and open discovery; separate discovery, mutation, verification, and action authority; classify out-of-scope findings; and preserve non-goals through continuation and delegation;
- child and nested-child scope is monotonically non-expanding, with root reconciliation against the actual diff and a negative oracle for unauthorized expansion;
- explicit cross-task synthesis, repeated-attempt comparison, reference-repository semantics, terminal-condition continuation, long-process supervision, temporal freshness, and confirmed-profile behavior have positive and negative deterministic contracts;
- the privacy-safe dogfood path accepts only authorized host reads or sanitized observations, emits no raw transcript, performs no automatic profile write, and keeps ordinary non-engineering conversations quiet;
- method-eligible verification/review/audit cases produce a ready-method, explicit fallback, or reasoned-abstention disposition; selected methods produce a decision-useful output; blocked-to-ready and review-to-verification cases work; and ordinary deterministic negative controls remain quiet;
- methodology evaluation reports activation, selection fit, realization, oracle/claim effect, regressions, and cost separately, while method-count and composite-score mutations fail;
- canonical RC.2/earlier RC.3 valid route behavior remains field-equivalent unless a separately documented additive contract is approved; no new public route field or persisted runtime state is required merely to express the scope envelope.

RC.3 is release-qualified only when the separately authorized R4 matrix additionally shows that representative implicit tasks activate on the first material turn, reroute at material transitions, keep narrow/unchanged negative controls quiet, isolate blocked capabilities honestly, realize warranted methods or explicit fallbacks without rewarding ceremony, invalidate stale evidence, keep deep implementation inside its admitted scope, preserve constraints through compaction/delegation, and handle explicit cross-task and long-process scenarios without overreach; an isolated RC.3 installation demonstrates the expected Skill inventory and routing behavior; and remaining host/tool limits are reported as `BLOCKED` or `NOT RUN` rather than inferred.

RC.3 release qualification does not require product release-pilot evidence. Stable `2.0.0` consideration additionally requires separately authorized real-task soak and the product-owned release-orchestration evidence described above.
